Quality & Paperwork

What ships with every container

Buyers rarely lose money on the price per ton. They lose it on documents that do not match the LC, or on a batch that left the plant with a different available chlorine than the one on the COA. These are the documents we issue, and how to read them.

01 · Certificate of Analysis (COA)

Issued per batch, not per year. Each COA carries the batch number, production date, test date, the test method used and the measured value — for calcium hypochlorite the available chlorine figure must come from iodometric titration to GB/T 10666, not from a "purity" claim.

02 · Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS / SDS)

16-section GHS format, in English, matching the UN number and packing group of the grade you buy. If the SDS says UN 2880 and the drum says nothing, stop and ask.

03 · Dangerous Goods documents

Dangerous goods declaration, UN packaging certificate (危包证), and the packing certificate for the container. Required for oxidizers and corrosives; missing paperwork at the port costs more than the goods.

04 · Commercial documents

Invoice, packing list with net/gross weights per drum, certificate of origin (CCPIT or CIQ), and — when the payment term is a letter of credit — documents prepared exactly to the LC wording, including full set of original bills of lading where the LC asks for 3/3 originals.

Every specification on this site is a typical value taken from routine production. The controlling number is the one on the COA of the batch you receive. Ask for it before you pay, and re-test on arrival.
